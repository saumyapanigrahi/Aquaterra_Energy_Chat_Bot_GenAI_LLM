1. Scope of Work
• The Contractor shall provide engineering, procurement, fabrication, installation support, testing and documentation services
associated with the offshore platform engineering & fabrication contract.
• The work covers the assets and project area identified as Aberdeen, UK / North Sea Block A17. The Contractor shall maintain
a project execution plan, quality plan, inspection and test plan, and document register.
• Any work outside the defined scope requires a written Variation Order approved by authorised representatives of both parties.
Verbal instructions do not constitute an approved change.
• The platform package includes topside structural fabrication, equipment integration support and engineering documentation for
offshore installation.
2. Commercial Terms
• The agreed contract value is USD 48,600,000. Unless expressly identified as reimbursable, the price includes labour,
supervision, standard tools, project management, engineering documentation and ordinary overheads.
• Invoices shall include the contract number, purchase order reference where applicable, milestone or service period, supporting
evidence and applicable taxes. The Client may reject an invoice that is incomplete or materially inconsistent with the supporting
records.
• Payment shall be made within 30 calendar days of receipt and acceptance of a valid invoice. Disputed amounts may be
withheld while undisputed amounts remain payable.
3. Milestones and Deliverables
• Key dates run from 15 Jan 2026 to 30 Sep 2028. The Contractor shall provide monthly progress reports, a
forecast-to-complete, risk register, action log and updated schedule.
• Typical milestone payments are: 10% mobilisation, 20% engineering approval, 30% procurement/fabrication progress, 25%
mechanical completion, and 15% final acceptance, subject to contract-specific evidence.
• Final acceptance requires completion of punch-list items, submission of as-built documentation, certificates and agreed
close-out records.
4. Performance, KPIs and Service Levels
• The Contractor shall track schedule performance, safety performance, quality NCRs, inspection completion, document
turnaround and milestone achievement.
• A monthly KPI review shall classify performance as On Track, At Risk or Off Track. Repeated failure to meet agreed KPIs may
trigger a corrective action plan.
• For service interruption or missed critical milestones, the Contractor must notify the Client within 24 hours and provide a
recovery plan within three business days.
5. Quality and Inspection
• All deliverables shall comply with the project specifications, approved drawings and applicable industry standards identified in
the project quality plan.
• The Client may inspect work and records on reasonable notice. Inspection does not relieve the Contractor of responsibility for
quality or contractual compliance.
• Non-conformances shall be logged, root cause assessed where appropriate, corrected and closed with objective evidence.
6. HSE Requirements
• The Contractor shall comply with applicable health, safety and environmental requirements and maintain a site-specific HSE
plan.
• Recordable incidents, environmental releases and high-potential events shall be notified to the Client without undue delay and
formally reported within 24 hours.
• Personnel shall hold required competency, certification and offshore medical/training documentation before mobilisation.

7. Change Control
• A Variation Order shall identify the requested change, reason, cost impact, schedule impact, assumptions and approval
authority.
• Approved variations shall be incorporated into the contract value and baseline schedule. Emergency work may proceed only
where authorised under the emergency instruction procedure.
• The Contractor shall maintain a variation register showing status, requested value, approved value and cumulative contract
impact.
8. Delay and Liquidated Damages
• If a Contractor-responsible delay affects a contractual milestone, the Client may require a recovery plan and may apply
liquidated damages of 0.10% of the affected milestone value per calendar week, capped at 10% of the total contract value.
• Liquidated damages are intended as a contractual remedy for specified delay and do not apply to delays caused by approved
variations, Client-caused delay or qualifying force majeure events.
• The Contractor must promptly notify the Client of events that may affect critical-path dates.
9. Liability, Insurance and Indemnity
• Except for excluded liabilities, aggregate liability of the Contractor shall not exceed 100% of the contract value.
• Excluded liabilities include fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal injury caused by negligence, and third-party intellectual
property infringement where expressly covered by the indemnity provisions.
• The Contractor shall maintain employer liability, public liability, professional indemnity and project-specific insurance at levels
stated in the insurance schedule.
10. Confidentiality and Data
• Commercial, technical, operational and project information exchanged under the contract shall be treated as confidential and
used only for contract performance.
• Project data shall be stored in authorised systems with access restricted to personnel with a legitimate business need.
• Confidentiality obligations survive contract expiry for five years, except where a longer period is required by law or for protected
intellectual property.
11. Termination
• Either party may terminate for material breach if the breach is not remedied within 30 days after written notice.
• The Client may terminate for convenience on 60 days written notice. In such case, the Contractor shall be paid for accepted
work performed and properly documented demobilisation costs, subject to the contract terms.
• Immediate termination may apply for insolvency, fraud or serious HSE misconduct where permitted by applicable law.
12. Force Majeure and Disputes
• A force majeure event means an exceptional event beyond reasonable control that prevents performance and is not caused by
the affected party's negligence.
• The affected party shall notify the other party within seven days and use reasonable efforts to mitigate the impact.
• Disputes shall first be referred to project management representatives, then senior management. If unresolved, disputes shall
be finally resolved by arbitration in London under the agreed arbitration rules.
13. Governing Law
• The governing law for this contract is the law of England and Wales, unless a project-specific schedule expressly states
otherwise.
• Nothing in this section prevents the parties from agreeing written amendments to applicable dispute procedures.
